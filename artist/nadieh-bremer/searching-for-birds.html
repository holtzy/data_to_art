Searching for Birds

An interactive story about the birds that capture our curiosity, told through data, visuals and illustrations.

A project by Nadieh BremerNadieh Bremer

Published on February 1, 2026

“Searching for Birds” was created for Google Trends with a clear design challenge: how can abstract search data be transformed into a visually rich, immersive experience that invites exploration?

The project analyzes large-scale Google Trends data about how people search for birds, enriched with publicly available bird datasets such as eBird sightings and population counts. Together, they reveal how people connect with birds through technology, curiosity and search behavior.

The opening of the story.

Instead of conventional charts, the project uses bespoke, creative data visualizations intertwined with illustration: birds' nests, eggs, feathers, and the birds themselves.

The color system, typography and interaction details were chosen to make the whole experience feel warm, whimsical and fun. The story, written by Emily Barone, focuses on the connection between humans and birds, and was crafted to be engaging and easy to follow.

The most Googled bird in each U.S. state.

Subtle motion and transitions create a feeling of “wandering”, encouraging curiosity and the discovery of birds at various locations throughout the story.

Search interest for the Snowy Owl, one of the birds that sparks sudden waves of curiosity.

The result is a cohesive visual world where story, data, illustration and interface are inseparable.
